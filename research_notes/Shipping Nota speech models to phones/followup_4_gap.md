# Can Ethiopian phones run Hohe? RAM share of active Android phones and iPhones in Ethiopia, 2025–2026

Research date: 6 November 2026. "Accessed" means read on that date. The figures Nota's own docs give for Hohe's memory:

- `mobile/README.md`: "Transcribing needs about 2 GB of free memory."
- `docs/embedded-speech.md`: "Loaded and idle, the 8-bit model takes about 0.9 GB and the 4-bit one about 0.6 GB." The desktop worker's peak memory, model included, is 1.8 GB on ~11 s clips and 2.9 GB on ~27 s pieces (8-bit, arena off; 2.0 / 3.1 GB at optimisation level 4). The phone runs 20 s pieces.

## What share of active Android phones in Ethiopia (and the iPhones in use there) have enough RAM to run Hohe? Is there device-share data for 3, 4 and 6 GB phones in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
No public primary source gives a RAM breakdown (3 / 4 / 6 GB) for phones in Ethiopia, or anywhere in East Africa, for 2025–2026. The newest free country-level RAM data (DeviceAtlas) is from Q2 2019 and leaves Ethiopia out. Analyst RAM-tier data (Counterpoint, IDC, Omdia) is paywalled. The proxies point to 4 GB as the usual floor of phones on sale now, with an older tail below it. StatCounter (October 2026) has Ethiopia at 93.8% Android and 6.0% iOS, with Samsung at 57% of mobile page views. About 70% of Android page views come from Android 13–16 and about 16% from Android 11–12. Budget Tecno models listed in Ethiopia ship with 4 GB of physical RAM, and Google's 2025 rules (secondary reports) push 2–3 GB phones onto Android Go. The 8-bit model's ~2 GB of free memory is a tight fit on a 4 GB phone and comfortable on 6 GB or more, so a per-device check is needed either way. The only real way to get the 3/4/6 GB split for Ethiopia is Google Play Console's Reach and devices view.

- Omdia (via search summaries of secondary write-ups): Transsion was Africa's leading smartphone vendor in 2025 with 44% share, and Omdia forecasts the African market to shrink about 23% in 2026. — [Intelpoint](https://intelpoint.co/blogs/mobile-phone-market-share-in-africa-by-region/); [digitalmag.ci](https://digitalmag.ci/afrique-844-millions-de-smartphones-ont-ete-livres-en-2025-omdia/?amp=1) (2026, secondary)

**iPhones**
- Support status (Wikipedia's list, accessed 6 Nov 2026): iPhone 8/8 Plus stop at iOS 16.7.x; iPhone XS/XS Max stop at iOS 18.7.x; iPhone 11, SE (2nd gen) and later run iOS 27.2. — [Wikipedia, List of iPhone models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_iPhone_models) (accessed 6 Nov 2026; secondary)
- Apple doesn't publish iPhone RAM; the figures come from teardowns. iPhone 8 Plus, X and XR: 3 GB. XS and the 11 series: 4 GB. iPhone 14 / 14 Pro Max: 6 GB. iPhone 16e and 17: 8 GB. These come from secondary lists; base iPhone 16 at 8 GB wasn't confirmed in what I read. — [iOSHacker RAM list](https://ioshacker.com/iphone/how-much-ram-does-my-iphone-has), [Islabit](https://www.islabit.com/en/171134/the-ram-memory-of-all-iphone-models-revealed.html), [iPhonesCompare](https://iphonescompare.com/iphone-ram-comparison), [9to5Mac RAM list](https://9to5mac.com/2023/11/18/iphone-ram-list/) (secondary; dates vary, 2023–2026)
- iOS caps each app's memory below the device's RAM, and Apple publishes no per-device numbers. The `com.apple.developer.kernel.increased-memory-limit` entitlement says an app's core features may work better with a higher limit "on supported devices", and the higher limit is available only on some models. Apple points to `os_proc_available_memory` for checking headroom. That function returns 0 when the process is over its limit, and Apple says not to use it to push memory use as high as possible. Apps must still work without the extra memory. — [Apple, increased-memory-limit entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.kernel.increased-memory-limit.md); [Apple, os_proc_available_memory](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/os/os_proc_available_memory.md) (current docs, accessed 6 Nov 2026)
- On Apple's developer forums, a developer said enabling the entitlement didn't change `os_proc_available_memory` on an iPhone 8. Apple DTS replied that the behaviour depends on the device type, the OS version and the device's state. The entitlement isn't in Xcode's capabilities editor; it is enabled as a capability in Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 740831](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/740831) (2023; **pre-2024**, still the documented mechanism)

**Where the real Ethiopia RAM split can be found**
- Play Console's "Reach and devices" shows install base, crash rate and ANR rate broken down by Android version, RAM, SoC, OpenGL ES/Vulkan version and screen. It is built for "minspec and country targeting" decisions, and can show your own app and peer sets you choose, with historical trends and CSV export. I found this only in Google's Medium posts and an archived dashboards page, not in current help docs. I couldn't confirm that a country-by-RAM cross-filter exists. — [Google Play (Medium): Better stats for better decisions](https://medium.com/googleplaydev/better-stats-for-better-decisions-3661717b4f2d); [archived Android dashboards page](https://apis.emri.workers.dev/https-web.archive.org/web/20240422020510/https://developer.android.com/about/dashboards) (2021–2024)
- Play AI packs and device targeting can send a different model variant, or none, by RAM (`min_bytes`/`max_bytes` device groups), SoC or device model. This is covered in the sibling note — see `store_native_delivery.md` (sections on AI packs and device targeting).

### Inferences
- **The phones that matter are Android, and mostly mid-range Samsung and budget Transsion.** On web traffic, about 94 of every 100 Ethiopian phones are Android and about 6 are iPhones. Even a perfect iOS story covers only a small slice of users.
- **Android version as an age proxy:** about 59% of Android page views come from Android 14–16 and about 70% from Android 13–16. About 16% come from Android 11–12, and up to about 14% from older or unlisted versions. A phone on Android 14+ was usually released around 2022 or later (launched with 12–14 and updated, or newer). Budget phones from that period mostly ship with 3–4 GB (Transsion) or 4–6 GB (Samsung A-series). The Android 11–12-and-older group (roughly 25–30%) is the likeliest home of 2–3 GB phones. I have no source that maps Android version to RAM in Ethiopia; this is a reasoned guess.
- **Rough planning bands, not measured:** on the proxies above, the likely picture is a large share of Ethiopian Android phones at about 4 GB, a minority at 6 GB or more (mostly Samsung A-series and newer mid-range phones), and a meaningful tail of perhaps a quarter at 3 GB or less. These bands are a judgement from proxies; no source gives the split.
- **What that means for Hohe on a 4 GB phone:** Android and the resident apps usually leave well under 2 GB free for a foreground app on a 4 GB phone (I found no source with a number). The 8-bit model needs about 0.9 GB idle. Its peak on 20 s pieces, by interpolation between the desktop's 1.8 GB (11 s) and 2.9 GB (27 s), is around 2.3–2.5 GB. So on many 4 GB phones the 8-bit model at 20 s pieces is likely to be killed or to page heavily. On 6 GB or more it should fit. Options that probably make 4 GB phones workable: the 4-bit model (0.6 GB idle), shorter pieces (memory grows with the square of piece length in attention), or both. This should be tested on a real 4 GB device (e.g. a Tecno Spark or Galaxy A05/A06 class phone) before deciding.
- **"4+4 GB" marketing:** Transsion's extended RAM is storage-backed swap. It doesn't add memory a model's weights can use efficiently, so treat such phones as 4 GB.
- **Google's Go rules help with detection:** if the April 2025 reports are right, phones that launched with Android 15 on 2–3 GB run Go edition, where `ActivityManager.isLowRamDevice()` is true. That flag, together with `ActivityManager.MemoryInfo.totalMem`, gives a cheap in-app check before offering the 840 MB download. Older 2–3 GB phones that weren't Go devices still need the `totalMem` check.
- **iPhones:** iPhones on iOS 26 or 27 (at least 52.8% of Ethiopian iOS page views) are iPhone 11 or newer, so 4 GB or more; about 2.6 points of the 6% iOS share in all. iOS 18.7 users (9.7%, some of them misreported iOS 26 devices) include XS (4 GB) and XR (3 GB). iOS 15.8 users (3.8%) can't install Nota (iOS 16+). The iPhone 8 (2 GB per common teardown reports; not confirmed in sources read here), 8 Plus, X and XR (3 GB) are the risky models. On a 3 GB iPhone the app's limit is very likely below the ~2 GB Hohe needs at 20 s pieces. An `os_proc_available_memory` check before loading is the iOS equivalent of the RAM gate, and the increased-memory-limit entitlement is worth requesting.
- **The trend is not improving quickly:** DRAM prices more than doubled in late 2025 to early 2026, and memory is now over 64% of the bill of materials for sub-$99 phones. Entry-level shipments in MEA fell 41% in Q1 2026. New low-end phones reaching Ethiopia in 2026–2027, including the GSMA ~$40 pilot devices, are likely to stay at 2–4 GB, or Ethiopians will keep their older phones longer. The low-RAM tail won't shrink fast.
- **Decision implication:** a large enough share of Ethiopian users is probably below what the 8-bit model needs that the 840 MB download should be gated by device. That means a Play AI pack RAM group, an in-app `totalMem` / `os_proc_available_memory` check before downloading, or both. A 4-bit (427 MB) variant for 4 GB phones should be considered, because 4 GB looks like the most common tier. An opt-in server path, or no speech, would cover phones under 4 GB. The real split should come from Play Console Reach and devices once the app has installs. Those statistics come from Google Play, not from Nota, so collecting them doesn't break the on-device promise.

### Gaps
- **No 2025–2026 RAM distribution for Ethiopia from any primary or free source.** DeviceAtlas's public country list stops at 2019 and leaves Ethiopia out. Counterpoint, IDC, Omdia and GSMA Intelligence RAM-tier data is paywalled. ScientiaMobile/DeviceAtlas commercial data might have it for a fee.
- **No Ethiopia-specific shipment shares for 2025–2026** (newest public: Counterpoint Q1 2021), and no list of best-selling models in Ethiopia. Marketplace listings give specs and prices but not volumes.
- **No Ethiopia smartphone-adoption figure** (only sub-Saharan Africa's 54% in 2024).
- **StatCounter's caveats:** it measures page views on sites carrying its tracker. That over-weights frequent web users, cities and newer phones, and under-weights feature-phone and low-data users. Its "Unknown" vendor bucket swings between about 7% and 22% month to month. Its Android version page shows only the top six versions.
- **Unverified Android 16 claim** (4 GB phones must ship Go edition): the GSMArena article was paywalled, and Google doesn't publish GMS hardware requirements. The Android 15 thresholds rest only on secondary reports.
- **No source for how much memory a foreground app actually gets** on a 4 GB Android phone or a 3–4 GB iPhone. Apple publishes no per-device app memory limits. The phone figure ("about 2 GB of free memory") is Nota's own measurement, and the 20 s peak is interpolated from desktop numbers.
- **iPhone RAM rests only on secondary teardown-based lists**; Apple doesn't publish it. The 2 GB iPhone 8 and the 8 GB base iPhone 16 weren't confirmed in what I read.
- **Play Console Reach and devices:** I couldn't confirm in current official help docs that RAM can be cross-filtered by country (Ethiopia) for peer sets before the app has its own installs.

### Inferences
- The decision has to rest on proxies plus Nota's own device testing, because no current primary source answers "what share has 4 GB or more" for Ethiopia. The proxies are consistent with each other: Android dominates, 4 GB is the floor of current budget models, and there is an older tail on Android 12 and below. Together they support gating by device rather than offering the 8-bit download to everyone.
- The single best primary source Nota can get is Play Console's Reach and devices, which gives RAM distributions for its own installs and peer sets. It needs a Play listing, even a closed or internal test track with some installs, before it shows Nota's own numbers.

### Gaps
- No primary source quotes a RAM share for Ethiopia for any year. The quotable primary material is limited to the StatCounter tables, Apple's memory-limit docs and Nota's own measurements.
- I didn't read StatCounter's methodology page this session, so the "page views on tracked sites" description rests on the general nature of StatCounter's data, not a quote.
- Google's GMS hardware requirements and Play Console's current Reach and devices help article weren't available as primary documents in this session.
