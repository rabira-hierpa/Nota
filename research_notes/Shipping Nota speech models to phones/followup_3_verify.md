# Verify: does an app update re-download an unchanged plain PAD fast-follow / on-demand pack?

Claim under test, from store_native_delivery.md (section 2 Inferences and section 6): "Play's patching re-downloads only changed files, [so] shipping app updates while leaving the model unchanged should cost users little data", and "Updated with app releases, with patching so unchanged packs aren't re-downloaded". I tried to refute it: I looked for any primary source saying an app update re-downloads plain asset packs in full, and for any primary source that says outright that it doesn't. Research date: 6 November 2026.

**Verdict: not established in so many words for plain asset packs, but strongly implied. No source points the other way.** Only the beta AI-packs page says outright that an unchanged pack isn't downloaded again. The plain PAD page documents a mechanism that only makes sense if the old pack stays on the device and a delta is applied to it: a patch is applied locally to the files already on disk, and patching "depend[s] on the integrity of these files". I found no primary source saying an unchanged plain pack is downloaded again in full. Section 6 of store_native_delivery.md says the plain PAD page supports "unchanged packs aren't re-downloaded". That overstates what the page says. The statement should be credited to the AI-packs page, with the plain-PAD case marked as inferred.

## With a plain Play Asset Delivery fast-follow or on-demand pack (not an AI pack), does an app update that leaves the 723 MB pack unchanged make users download it again? Or does Play's patching skip it, as the AI-packs page says for AI packs?

### Takeaway
No primary Google source says that an unchanged plain asset pack costs nothing to update. No primary source says it is downloaded again either. The documented PAD update flow is "download a patch, invalidate, apply the patch locally to the files in internal storage", sold as "delta patching". That flow, plus the fact that AI packs are built as ordinary asset packs (same `assetPacks` Gradle property, same delivery modes, a mirrored client API), makes "unchanged pack ≈ near-zero update download" the likely behaviour. It stays an inference until Nota measures it on a Play test track.

### Cited Findings
**Plain PAD overview (primary; page "Last updated 2026-10-06 UTC")**
- PAD "offers flexible delivery modes, auto-updates, compression, and delta patching, and is free to use." Elsewhere: "Let Play auto-update your game assets with advanced compression and delta patching". — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)
- Install-time packs: "When the app is updated, `install-time` asset packs are updated as part of the base app update (with no action needed from the developer)." — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)
- Fast-follow and on-demand packs, verbatim update steps:
  1. "The patch for the app, including all assets, is downloaded to a secure location on the device."
  2. "The app binary is updated; this includes any `install-time` asset packs."
  3. "All previously-downloaded asset packs are invalidated."
  4. "The patch for the assets is copied and applied to assets stored in the app's internal storage."

  — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)
- "Since the app binary update takes place only after all asset pack types have been downloaded, applying the patch is a local, offline action that should complete quickly." — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)
- On files in fast-follow and on-demand packs: "Even though these files are writable by the app, you should treat them as read-only since asset pack patches depend on the integrity of these files." — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)
- Apps must handle the window in which "the app binary may have already been updated while the process of applying the patch for the assets has not yet completed and thus assets are not ready to be accessed", for example with an "Update in progress" UI, "or build in logic to deal with invalidated assets". — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)
- The page has no sentence on whether a pack whose contents didn't change is skipped, or on how large a patch is. (Checked by full-page extraction, Nov 2026.) — [Android Developers: Play Asset Delivery](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery)

**Plain PAD integration guide (primary; no date shown; uses asset-delivery 2.3.0)**
- "Do not rely on cached asset pack locations between app launches. The app should always check for the existence of asset packs at every launch. Asset packs may become invalid due to app updates or if the user clears the app data." — [Android Developers: Integrate asset delivery (Kotlin & Java)](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery/integrate-java)
- `fetch()` is used "to download an asset pack for the first time or call for the update of an asset pack to complete". "The Play Store automatically triggers the download of any `fast-follow` packs after the user installs or updates the app." — [Android Developers: Integrate asset delivery (Kotlin & Java)](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery/integrate-java)
- `getPackLocations()` "contains an entry for each pack that is currently downloaded and up-to-date". So Play tracks whether each pack is up to date. — [Android Developers: Integrate asset delivery (Kotlin & Java)](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery/integrate-java)
- The page says nothing on update download size for unchanged packs. — [same](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery/integrate-java)

**Google's Unity PAD API reference (primary, undated)**
- `AssetPackUpdateAvailability` is an "Enum indicating the availability of a newer version of a particular asset pack". Its values: `UpdateAvailable`, "The asset pack can be updated to a newer version", and `UpdateNotAvailable`, "The asset pack is already at the latest version; there's no update available". So Play versions each pack separately, not only the app. — [Android Developers: Google.Play.AssetDelivery namespace (Unity)](https://developer.android.com/reference/unity/namespace/Google/Play/AssetDelivery)
- A search-result summary of Google's Unity docs says `RetrieveAssetPackAsync()` downloads a pack only "if the latest version of the pack isn't already available on disk". I did not confirm the exact wording by fetching the page. — [Android Developers: Integrate asset delivery (Unity)](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery/integrate-unity?language=api)

**Original PAD announcement (primary, Android Developers Blog, 9 June 2020, Dan Galpin; older than 2024)**
- "When your app auto updates, the entire game gets updated, including all assets." "Play also takes care of delta patching for you, minimizing the download size and the update time." Also announced as generally available: "Play Asset Delivery … along with its three delivery modes and updates + patching are all generally available." The post doesn't say whether unchanged packs are skipped. — [Android Developers Blog: Introducing Google Play Asset Delivery (2020)](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2020/06/introducing-google-play-asset-delivery.html)

**Google case study (primary, Google-published, undated; covers 2020 events, so older than 2024)**
- CD Projekt RED's GWENT ships "over 40 asset packs that range in size from 4 MB to 160 MB each" as fast-follow. Thanks to "Play Asset Delivery auto-updates and delta patching … CDPR was able to reduce their update size by 90%. An update that used to require download and extra storage space of more than 2GB now only takes a couple hundred MB." This is evidence of small updates with many fast-follow packs. It doesn't say which packs changed, so it can't show that an unchanged pack costs zero. — [Android Developers: CD Projekt RED story](https://developer.android.com/stories/games/cdpr)

**AI packs (primary; page titled "beta" in Nov 2026; no date extracted)**
- "AI packs are updated together with the app binary. If your new app release doesn't make changes to an AI pack, then Play's automatic patching process will ensure the user doesn't have to re-download it. Play will just download what's changed when it updates the app." Also: "Keep app updates small and optimized with Play's automatic patching, which means only the differences in files need to be downloaded." — [Android Developers: Play for On-device AI](https://developer.android.com/google/play/on-device-ai)
- AI packs are wired into the bundle the same way as plain asset packs. The app's build.gradle lists them in the same property, `assetPacks = [":ai-pack-name", ...]`. They have the same three delivery modes and the same "stored in a separate folder in the app's internal storage" layout for fast-follow and on-demand. Their client API (`AiPackManager`: `getPackStates`, `fetch`, `getPackLocation`, `showConfirmationDialog`, the same status names) mirrors `AssetPackManager`. — [Android Developers: Play for On-device AI](https://developer.android.com/google/play/on-device-ai); compare [Integrate asset delivery (Kotlin & Java)](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/asset-delivery/integrate-java)
- AI packs also say: "AI packs may become invalid due to app updates or if the user clears the app data". The plain PAD guide carries the same warning. — [Android Developers: Play for On-device AI](https://developer.android.com/google/play/on-device-ai)

- Searches for a statement that PAD re-downloads unchanged packs in full found none, on Google or elsewhere.

### Inferences
- **The documented mechanism points against a full re-download.** Step 4 applies "the patch for the assets" to the assets already in internal storage. Google also warns that patches "depend on the integrity of these files". Both make sense only if the update is a delta computed against the bytes on the device. If Play re-sent whole packs, the old files' integrity wouldn't matter. "Invalidated" (step 3) reads as "temporarily not usable until patched", not "deleted and downloaded again".
- **AI packs are almost certainly the same pipeline.** They go in the same `assetPacks` slot of the bundle, with the same modes, storage layout and API shape. The AI-packs page names the patching process as Play's "automatic patching process", not as an AI-pack feature. So its explicit statement very likely describes general PAD behaviour that the older PAD page never spelled out. This is an inference: no Google page says "AI packs use the same patching as asset packs".
- **For Nota, then, the likely cost of an app update that leaves the 723 MB pack byte-identical is a small patch, not 840 MB.** I found no primary source that refutes this. The fear that "an ordinary app update can cost Ethiopian users 840 MB" isn't supported by any source. It isn't ruled out in writing for plain packs either.
- **"Byte-identical" is the condition that matters.** Delta patching saves data only if the pack bytes don't change. If Nota re-exports or re-quantizes the ONNX files, even with the same weights (a new ORT or optimum version, different node ordering), the file changes. Quantized weights also compress and diff poorly: docs/embedded-speech.md says quantized weights compress only 5–7%. Such a release would likely cost close to the full pack size. Nota should keep the model files in the pack frozen, for example checked by hash in CI, and change them only in deliberate model releases.
- **Splitting packs limits the damage of a model change.** With Hohe and punctuation in separate packs (723 MB and 117 MB), a punctuation-only change touches only the smaller pack. With a single pack, delta patching should still confine the download to the changed file, but that is less certain.
- **Even if patching works, there are cases where users pay the full size again:** clearing app data (packs become invalid per the integration guide), uninstalling and reinstalling, or a corrupted file that the patch can't apply to. What Play does in the last case isn't documented, and a full re-download is the plausible fallback. These costs fall the same way on a self-hosted download, so they don't favour either option.
- **Nota must handle the invalidation window regardless.** After an update, `getPackLocation()` can return null until the local patch finishes. The app must re-check pack location at every launch and show "updating speech model" instead of reporting the model as missing.
- **How to settle it for real (cheap):** publish v1 with a fast-follow pack to an internal test track and install it from Play. Then publish v2 with only a Dart change and the pack untouched. Read `AssetPackState.totalBytesToDownload()` and the Play Store update size, or watch network usage. bundletool local testing can't do this ("Updates are not supported"). The AI-packs page recommends testing "end to end on Google Play with an internal test track".
- **Effect on the PAD vs self-hosted comparison:** on the evidence, PAD does not lose to self-hosting on update cost for unchanged models. The real differences remain that model updates can't happen without an app release (PAD) and that Nota gets explicit control over versions (self-hosted).

### Gaps
- No primary source states, for plain (non-AI) asset packs, that an unchanged fast-follow or on-demand pack isn't downloaded again or costs a near-zero patch. The only explicit statement is on the beta AI-packs page.
- No Google source gives patch sizes, the patching algorithm for asset packs (file-level, block-level, or a per-pack hash skip), or what happens when the on-device files fail an integrity check.
- Not documented: whether the post-update patch download for fast-follow and on-demand packs goes through the 200 MB "wait for Wi-Fi / ask on mobile data" gate, or rides along with the Play Store's own auto-update rules.
- Google's 2016 work on file-by-file patching of APK updates (reported as a 65% reduction) is known only from secondary coverage found in search (for example BleepingComputer). It concerns APKs, not asset packs, and predates 2024. I did not fetch or rely on it.
- The AI-packs page's last-updated date could not be extracted. The plain PAD page's date is 2026-10-06. The integration guide and Unity reference show no dates. The 2020 blog post and the CDPR story predate 2024, so their details may have changed, although the 2026 PAD page still describes the same patch flow.
